Meet One Spread, a paper-only SPY options agent built by a solo quant developer. It makes the evidence, the model proposal, and the execution decision inspectable.

An options trading agent can give a convincing explanation while relying on prices that have already changed. One Spread focuses on that gap between a proposal and an order.

The strategy is deliberately narrow: bull call or bear put debit spreads, seven to fourteen days to expiry, with a five-point strike width and one contract per leg.

Only one spread can be active, with at most three hundred dollars of entry premium before costs. This chart is a hypothetical expiration payoff, not realized performance. Assignment and execution complications remain.

Here is the actual dashboard, showing a synthetic replay. The model decision is scripted and the broker is simulated. The replay runs through the same execution engine used by the paper agent.

In the real integration, the official Alpaca command-line tool supplies market and account reads. G L M five runs through Featherless and proposes an eligible spread, or chooses to wait.

The model cites supplied evidence, including a simple momentum classification and news headlines. It cannot call the broker, set position size, or override the numerical rules.

After inference, code refreshes quotes, context, and account state. It checks price deterioration, existing exposure, quote freshness, and reviewed event-calendar coverage before allowing entry.

In a small synthetic integration evaluation, the selected hosted model configuration passed ten of ten cases, with a median response time of about thirty-nine seconds. That latency made refreshing the evidence essential.

Now look at the stale-quote scenario. The quote is too old, so the execution gate returns WAIT with an explicit reason. No new order is authorized.

A clear refusal is part of the product. This result is reproducible without credentials, and it does not depend on trusting the model to enforce the risk limit.

The next replay follows a simulated order lifecycle. Before sending a paper order, the engine commits a durable intent to its local journal.

If an acknowledgement is lost, it reconciles the broker state instead of blindly resubmitting. An accepted order is still not a confirmed fill.

Once the owned spread is confirmed, subsequent cycles check the account and manage the position. Unexpected positions and partial fills require operator attention.

The engine can submit a closing limit when its exit conditions are reached. A limit order may remain unfilled, so the process must stay running to manage its state.

Completion requires a confirmed exit fill and a flat account. All the broker transitions shown here are simulated, and do not prove an actual paper round trip.

The decision journal lets a reviewer follow the sequence and its recorded reasons. The local dashboard is read-only: refreshing it does not place a trade.

What has been exercised? Alpaca read access, paid Featherless inference, twenty-two offline tests, and three synthetic replay scenarios. Real broker submission and fills remain unverified.
